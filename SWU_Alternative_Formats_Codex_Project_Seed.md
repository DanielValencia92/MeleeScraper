# SWU Alternative Formats Research — Codex Project Seed

_Last updated from the data and discussion in this conversation._

## Purpose

This document consolidates the quantitative summaries, observations, hypotheses, and caveats discussed so far regarding Star Wars: Unlimited competitive format participation, with emphasis on:

- Limited Planetary Qualifiers (PQs)
- Eternal Planetary Qualifiers
- Larger Eternal Open / Sector Open events
- Geographic availability of Premier, Limited, and Eternal PQs
- The distinction between **format popularity** and **format accessibility**

The goal is to compartmentalize the current state of the analysis before moving into deeper geographic, accessibility, and demand modeling.

# 1. Limited PQ Attendance by Set

## Summary

| Set | Events | Avg. Players | Sample SD | Median | Approx. Player-Entries |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LOF | 10 | 90.4 | 27.4 | 86 | 904 |
| SEC | 40 | 45.1 | 16.8 | 43.5 | 1,804 |
| LAW | 37 | 44.1 | 19.1 | 43 | 1,632 |
| ASH | 43 | 43.1 | 18.2 | 42 | 1,853 |

Approximate player-entries are calculated as:

`Events × Average Players`

This is **not unique players**. A player attending multiple events is counted multiple times.

## Main observations

### LOF appears structurally different from later sets

LOF had only 10 PQs but averaged 90.4 players per event.

From SEC onward, Limited PQ attendance appears much more stable:

- SEC: 45.1
- LAW: 44.1
- ASH: 43.1

This suggests Limited may have settled into a relatively stable equilibrium of roughly **43–45 players per PQ** after the initial LOF period.

### More Limited PQs did not meaningfully dilute average attendance

From LAW to ASH:

- Events increased from 37 to 43
- Average attendance decreased only from 44.1 to 43.1
- Approximate player-entries increased from 1,632 to 1,853

This is important because it suggests that increasing event supply does **not automatically cause severe event cannibalization**.

# 5. Why “Events / Average Players” Is Not Very Useful

A ratio such as:

`Number of Events / Average Attendance`

does produce a number, but the interpretation is weak.

A high value could mean:

- more events
- lower attendance
- both

It mixes supply and demand into a ratio without a natural real-world meaning.

Better metrics are:

- **Events** = breadth / availability
- **Average or median attendance** = typical event strength
- **Approx. player-entries** = overall participation scale
- **Standard deviation / coefficient of variation** = consistency
- **Set-to-set % delta** = trajectory
- **Format attendance ratio** = relative demand between formats

---

# 6. Coefficient of Variation

Coefficient of variation:

`CV = Standard Deviation / Mean`

This normalizes event-size variability relative to average event size.

| Format | Set | Mean | SD | CV |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Limited | LOF | 90.4 | 27.4 | 30.3% |
| Limited | SEC | 45.1 | 16.8 | 37.3% |
| Limited | LAW | 44.1 | 19.1 | 43.3% |
| Limited | ASH | 43.1 | 18.2 | 42.2% |
| Eternal | LAW | 30.3 | 14.2 | 46.9% |
| Eternal | ASH | 27.2 | 11.8 | 43.4% |

## Interpretation

Eternal events are smaller, but their relative variability is not wildly different from Limited.

This is notable because it suggests Eternal may already have a **fairly consistent underlying audience**, just at a lower attendance level.

# 9. Concentrated Demand vs. Weak Demand

This may be the most important conceptual distinction in the article.

A format can have:

- a substantial national or international audience
- but insufficient local density to create 40–50 player events everywhere

This leads to a useful question:

> Is Eternal unpopular, or simply geographically dispersed?

The current data is consistent with the possibility that Eternal has **concentrated demand**:

- modest local PQ fields
- much larger destination-event fields
- fewer available venues
- demand becoming visible when players are brought together

This is different from a format having no meaningful player interest.

---

# 10. Why the Large Eternal Events Matter

If Eternal had essentially no demand, it would be difficult to reconcile that with multiple events drawing:

- 126
- 126
- 127
- 175

These events also occurred in more than one major market:

- Las Vegas
- Berlin
- Indianapolis

This weakens the interpretation that one unusually strong local scene is solely responsible.

However, these events are **not directly comparable** to PQs because they are:

- attached to larger destination events
- one-day events
- often 6–7 rounds
- no cut
- accessible to players already traveling for other competitive activities

They should therefore be treated as evidence of a broader **reservoir of interest**, not as evidence that ordinary Eternal PQs should attract 100+ players.

# 11. Share of ASH Participation Represented by Major Events

Using the known ASH data:

## Eternal

- PQ entries: ~435
- Large-event entries: 554
- Combined represented entries: 989

Large events therefore account for approximately:

**554 / 989 ≈ 56%**

of the recorded ASH Eternal participation represented in the current dataset.

## Limited

- PQ entries: ~1,853
- Large-event entries: 788
- Combined represented entries: 2,641

Large events account for approximately:

**788 / 2,641 ≈ 30%**

of the represented Limited participation.

## Interpretation

Major-event access appears disproportionately important for Eternal in the current data.

This should **not** be interpreted as proof that Eternal players inherently prefer large events, because event schedules and availability differ substantially.

But it strongly suggests that access to major events may matter more for Eternal than for Limited.

# 12. Accessibility as a Potential Explanatory Variable

The main working hypothesis is:

> Eternal attendance may be constrained partly by where FFG chooses to offer Eternal events, rather than purely by player interest.

When a format is new, a publisher may reasonably be conservative in allocating events because it does not yet know:

- player demand
- organizer demand
- local player commitment
- deck ownership / readiness
- travel willingness

This can create a feedback loop:

`fewer events → less reason to maintain an Eternal deck → smaller local Eternal communities → lower attendance → fewer events`

The reverse is also possible:

`more accessible events → more incentive to build → stronger local communities → better attendance`

The existing attendance data cannot yet determine which equilibrium Eternal will reach.

# 15. Why Accessibility and Popularity Should Be Separated

Average attendance is partly a demand measure, but not purely.

Observed attendance is influenced by:

- player interest
- number of events
- geographic placement
- travel distance
- date conflicts
- venue capacity
- local scene strength
- format age
- player familiarity
- cost of maintaining a deck
- whether the event is attached to a larger tournament

Therefore:

> Low attendance does not automatically imply low latent demand.

Similarly:

> A small number of events does not prove demand is weak if access is highly constrained.

This motivates a deeper geographic analysis.

# 16. Promising Future Accessibility Metrics

The strongest next phase would be city-level or venue-level mapping.

Potential metrics include:

## Nearest-event distance

For each city / market:

- distance to nearest Premier PQ
- distance to nearest Limited PQ
- distance to nearest Eternal PQ

This would quantify whether Eternal requires substantially more travel.

## Events within a fixed radius

Examples:

- number of PQs within 100 km
- number within 250 km
- number within 500 km

This would measure practical format availability.

## Regional format share

For each country / region:

`Eternal PQs / Total PQs`

and

`Alternative-format PQs / Total PQs`

This highlights areas where format diversification is high or absent.

## Same-market comparison

Compare attendance in cities or metro areas that host multiple formats.

Example:

- Limited PQ attendance in City X
- Eternal PQ attendance in City X
- Premier PQ attendance in City X

This helps distinguish:

> “This market has a small SWU scene”

from:

> “This market specifically has low Eternal demand”

## Venue density

Events per:

- million population
- major metro
- country
- active competitive region

This would normalize event allocation by market size.

## Geographic concentration

Possible measures:

- percentage of Eternal PQs in top 5 countries
- Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)
- Gini-style concentration metrics

These would make claims about geographic concentration more rigorous.

# 17. Strong Current Article Framing

A defensible thesis based on the current data is:

> Eternal appears to have a smaller but nontrivial competitive audience. PQ attendance is roughly 60–70% of Limited attendance, while Eternal receives substantially fewer events and is far more geographically concentrated. Larger Eternal events can still attract fields well above 100 players, suggesting that the format's audience may be more geographically dispersed than weak. The available evidence therefore supports separating the question of Eternal's popularity from the question of Eternal's accessibility.

Another concise framing:

> The central question may not be “Is Eternal popular?” but “How much Eternal demand is invisible because players do not have nearby events?”

# 19. Important Caveats

These should remain explicit in any deeper article.

## Player-entries are not unique players

A player attending two events is counted twice.

## Event types differ

PQ, Sector Open, Galactic Open, Sector Qualifier, and Regional Championship events are not equivalent products.

Their:

- duration
- prestige
- travel draw
- prize structure
- qualification stakes
- location context

can differ.

## Eternal is newer

It has had less time for:

- deck ownership to spread
- local communities to organize
- organizers to learn expected demand
- FFG to adjust allocations
- players to decide whether maintaining an Eternal deck is worthwhile

## Availability is endogenous

FFG may allocate fewer Eternal events because it expects lower demand.

But lower event supply can itself suppress participation.

This makes simple causal interpretation difficult.

## Geography matters

Country totals are useful, but city-level distance and population density will be much more informative.

# 20. Current Working Questions for Deeper Analysis

1. Are Eternal events genuinely under-supplied relative to observed demand?
2. How much farther does the average player need to travel for Eternal than for Limited or Premier?
3. Are Eternal PQs disproportionately concentrated in a small number of countries or metro areas?
4. Do Eternal events perform better in markets that also receive strong Premier / Limited support?
5. Does Eternal attendance improve when it is attached to a larger destination event?
6. Are large Eternal events drawing a geographically broader audience than PQs?
7. Does more local event supply increase total participation without strongly cannibalizing average attendance?
8. Which regions appear to have plausible Eternal demand but no Eternal PQ access?
9. How does format allocation change from ASH to Homeworlds?
10. Can same-city comparisons isolate format preference from overall local SWU scene strength?

---

# 21. Current Bottom Line

The present data supports several reasonably strong observations:

- **Limited appears mature and stable** at roughly 43–45 players per PQ after LOF.
- **Eternal is smaller**, averaging roughly 27–30 players per PQ.
- Eternal nevertheless has a **substantial competitive audience**, demonstrated by repeated 100+ player events.
- **Large-event Eternal demand is much stronger than PQ averages alone suggest.**
- Eternal has **far fewer opportunities to play**, and those opportunities are highly geographically concentrated.
- This means **attendance should not be treated as a pure measure of popularity**.
- The next meaningful step is to quantify **accessibility, distance, and geographic concentration**.

The deeper question is no longer simply whether Eternal is popular.

It is whether its current tournament footprint accurately reflects the size and distribution of the audience that wants to play it.

# 22. Codex Project Objective

Build a reproducible local Python project that can collect public Star Wars: Unlimited event data, normalize it, geocode it, identify hubs of play by format, and compare those hubs against official Planetary Qualifier allocation.

The core research question is:

> **Does the geographic supply of alternative-format competitive events reflect the geographic demand visible in ordinary organized play?**

A related framing is:

> **Is Eternal currently unpopular, or is its player base more geographically dispersed and underserved by event allocation?**

The project should ultimately separate three different ideas that are easy to conflate:

- **Popularity** — how much people actually play a format.
- **Accessibility** — how close players are to opportunities to play it.
- **Event allocation** — where FFG / tournament organizers choose to place higher-tier events.

# 25. Recommended Scraping Architecture

## Stage A — Discovery

Find public SWU event IDs over bounded historical date windows.

Recommended strategy:

```text
monthly or quarterly windows
```

rather than one giant historical request.

Persist each discovered tournament ID so runs are resumable and deduplicated.

## Stage B — Raw Archive

For every public tournament page, cache:

```text
HTML
visible page text
page title
public links
relevant public XHR/fetch responses
```

Raw caching is important because parsing rules can be improved later without repeatedly scraping the source.

## Stage C — Normalization

Store parsed event rows in SQLite initially.

Prefer explicit missing values over guesses.

Examples:

```text
unknown format     -> Unknown
unknown attendance -> NULL
unknown city       -> NULL
```

## Stage D — Geocoding

Geocode at the **city / metro level** wherever possible.

Exact storefront coordinates are not required for the primary accessibility questions.

Cache geocoding results locally.

## Stage E — Analysis

Summarize play by:

```text
city / metro
country
format
month
venue
organizer
```

Then compare organic play against official PQ locations.

# 27. Data Quality / Biases

This future dataset measures **Melee-reported SWU activity**, not all SWU activity.

Potential biases:

- some stores do not use Melee,
- some stores use Melee inconsistently,
- registration-only events may represent signups rather than attendance,
- some events may be cancelled,
- event names and tags may be inconsistent,
- player counts may be absent,
- one player can generate many player-entries,
- different countries may prefer different event platforms,
- Eternal is newer and has less time to accumulate local habits.

All article conclusions should explicitly distinguish observed Melee activity from total real-world play.

# 28. Hub / Hotspot Analysis

Do **not** create one opaque hotspot score immediately.

First preserve the component metrics for each geographic cluster:

```text
event count
active weeks
active months
total known player-entries
median attendance
mean attendance
unique venues
unique organizers
format share
attendance-weighted format share

```

A market with forty 8-player weekly events is structurally different from one with six 50-player events, even if a single weighted score makes them look similar.

# 35. Causal Caution

Do not interpret correlation as causation.

Observation:

```text
Cities with more Eternal play tend to have nearby Eternal PQs.
```

Possible explanations include:

```text
FFG allocates PQs where Eternal is already popular.
```

or:

```text
PQ availability increases local Eternal activity.
```

or both.

Potential future causal approaches:

- before/after analysis,
- matched markets,
- difference-in-differences,
- tracking allocation changes across sets.

# 42. Important Statistical Questions

1. Does local same-format activity predict whether a PQ is allocated nearby?
2. Is Eternal PQ allocation more geographically concentrated than Limited / Premier?
3. Are Eternal hubs farther from same-format PQs than Limited hubs?
4. Is Eternal local activity growing after controlling for format age?
5. Does nearby PQ availability correlate with increased local same-format activity afterward?
6. Are alternative formats more dependent on destination events than Premier?
7. Does strong overall SWU activity predict alternative-format adoption?
8. Are there strong Eternal play hubs with no Eternal PQ nearby?
9. Does increasing local event supply grow total participation without materially reducing average attendance?
10. How stable are the same identified hubs across sets?

# 45. Current Research Thesis

The strongest current framing is:

> Eternal appears to have a smaller but nontrivial competitive audience. Its PQs average roughly 60–70% of Limited attendance, while the format receives substantially fewer events and is much more geographically concentrated. Larger Eternal events can still attract fields well above 100 players, suggesting that Eternal's audience may be more geographically dispersed than weak. The available evidence therefore supports separating the question of Eternal's popularity from the question of Eternal's accessibility.

The stronger future thesis, **only if supported by Melee data**, would be:

> Some regions have demonstrably healthy Eternal local play but little or no nearby Eternal PQ access, implying that observed PQ attendance understates latent demand in underserved markets.
